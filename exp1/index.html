Quantum Cryptography in Smart Campuses

Next-Generation Wireless Network Security

Published by: Apex Institute Research Wing • Last updated: July 2026

Modern smart campus networks face unprecedented security challenges due to the proliferation of IoT devices. Traditional encryption algorithms, such as RSA and ECC, are vulnerable to quantum computing attacks.
To safeguard wireless transmissions, researchers are looking toward Quantum Key Distribution (QKD) protocols.

The BB84 Protocol Mechanism

The BB84 protocol, designed by Charles Bennett and Gilles Brassard in 1984, leverages the fundamental laws of quantum physics. When Alice transmits polarized photons to Bob, any eavesdropper (Eve) attempting to intercept the photons will inevitably alter their states, thereby triggering an alert in the security system.

Key State Representation & Complexity

Quantum State Complexity: Ψtotal = α|0⟩Alice + β|1⟩Bob

Probability space for an n-qubit key: S = 2n combinations

By measuring the error rate of the sample qubits, Alice (represented as Akey) and Bob (represented as Bkey) can calculate if the information has been intercepted. If the eavesdropping threshold is exceeded, the key is discarded and a new quantum channel is negotiated.

Summary of Security Benefits

1. Absolute Eavesdropper Detection: Physics-based detection guarantees intrusion discovery.
2. Forward Secrecy: Compromise of current keys does not compromise past transmissions.
3. Future-Proof Security: Resilient against mathematically advanced computational machines (e.g., 2128 FLOP systems).
